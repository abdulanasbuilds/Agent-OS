# Design Brief

Do not start with a component list. First establish what the interface must accomplish.

## Question set

Ask only the highest-value missing questions, grouped so the user can answer quickly:

### Product
- What is the product/service?
- What is the primary business or user outcome?
- What is the primary call to action?

### Audience
- Who is the primary user/buyer?
- What do they already trust or expect from this category?
- What device and environment will they mainly use?

### Brand
- Existing brand identity or starting from zero?
- Personality: restrained, editorial, technical, warm, premium, playful, utilitarian, etc.?
- Any colors, fonts, logos, visual rules, or things explicitly disliked?

### Content and assets
- What content must be above the fold?
- Which images, videos, illustrations, icons, logos, or screenshots are authentic and available?
- Are there licensed/owned assets that must be used?

### Experience
- What actions must feel effortless?
- What parts deserve emphasis?
- What interactions or motion are actually useful?

## Output

Produce:
1. Design objective
2. Audience/context
3. Content hierarchy
4. Brand/visual direction
5. Typography direction
6. Color strategy
7. Layout strategy
8. Component strategy
9. Motion strategy
10. Asset strategy
11. Responsive/accessibility constraints
12. Open decisions

Do not fill missing answers with assumptions silently. Label assumptions and either ask for the missing information or create clearly marked variants.
