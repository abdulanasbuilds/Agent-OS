# Global Presentation Policy

Presentation work is a communication system, not a collection of decorated slides.

## Purpose first

Before designing a deck, identify:
- the decision, action, or understanding the audience should leave with
- audience, setting, duration, delivery mode, and distribution mode
- source material and evidence quality
- whether the deck is live, forwarded, self-read, recorded, printed, or exported
- business context and consequence of success/failure

Choose structure and density from those constraints.

## Narrative before styling

Do not start with a theme. Establish the core message, supporting claims, narrative arc, and slide sequence before visual treatment.

Every slide needs a job. Split slides when one slide carries multiple competing ideas.

## Distinctive without gimmicks

Do not default to template-looking decks, generic dark gradients, giant centered headlines, repeated card grids, random icons, decorative 3D, excessive glass, or motion without communicative purpose.

A deck may be minimal, editorial, technical, expressive, cinematic, consulting-style, academic, product-led, or otherwise distinctive when the choice fits the audience and message.

## Visual evidence

Prefer authentic product visuals, real screenshots, real diagrams, charts, relevant photography, and purposeful illustration. Do not fabricate factual metrics, people, testimonials, products, logos, or screenshots merely to fill space.

## Speaker mode versus reading mode

A live presentation can leave more explanation to the speaker. A forwarded or self-read deck needs more context. Choose density accordingly.

Speaker notes are cues and supporting detail, not a word-for-word script unless explicitly requested.

## Charts and data

Use the simplest visual form that makes the comparison, trend, distribution, relationship, or magnitude clear. Do not distort axes, hide material context, or decorate charts at the expense of interpretation.

## Accessibility

Give every slide a meaningful title, maintain logical reading order, provide useful alt text for meaningful visuals, do not use color as the only signal, maintain sufficient contrast, and provide captions/transcripts for relevant audio or video. Follow the target presentation platform's accessibility checker where available.

## Motion

Motion should establish continuity, focus attention, reveal structure, or communicate state. Prefer restrained transitions. Respect reduced-motion preferences in HTML/web presentation output and avoid motion that creates distraction or accessibility barriers.

## Variant lab

When visual direction is uncertain, create 2-4 materially different variants in an isolated lab. Compare them using the same criteria before promotion. Do not overwrite the main deck repeatedly while exploring.

## Output integrity

Prefer source-first generation. Maintain an editable source representation where practical. Export to PPTX, PDF, or HTML after the source passes structural and visual QA.

## External references

Presentation galleries, templates, screenshots, fonts, icons, images, animations, and components are reference material unless reuse rights are verified. Never treat public visibility as permission to copy proprietary work.
