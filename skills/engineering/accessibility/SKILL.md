# Accessibility

Treat accessibility as a functional requirement, not visual polish.

## Workflow
1. Identify the platforms, interaction modes, and supported assistive technologies.
2. Inspect semantic structure, names/labels, focus order, keyboard/touch access, and form/error behavior.
3. Check visual requirements such as contrast, text scaling, focus indication, and motion preferences.
4. Verify interactive states and dynamic content announcements where relevant.
5. Test representative flows with automated checks plus manual interaction.
6. Fix the smallest structural issue first; avoid cosmetic work that hides semantic problems.

## Safety
Do not remove accessible controls or reduce information density solely to make an interface look cleaner. Preserve equivalent access to important actions and information.

## Verification
Report the tested flows, platform/browser or device assumptions, automated findings, manual findings, and remaining limitations.
